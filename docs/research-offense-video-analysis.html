How to analyse one player's offense reel, and what to give him back

Post-game offense review for a pickup player: validated research report

Scope: one fixed sideline phone (1920x1080, 30 fps, players 100-160 px), an adult recreational player, a weekly 4-on-4 game, an existing YOLO11/ffmpeg pipeline that already produces a 27-clip offense reel. Seven research sweeps, 44 load-bearing claims fact-checked, one gap-fill round. Where a check downgraded a claim, the corrected version is used here. One caveat applies to the whole document: no study of adult recreational players coaching themselves from their own video was found; every feedback, practice-schedule and self-modeling result below comes from youth, novices, students or lab tasks, so the workflow is an extrapolation.

1. What the research says in one page

  1. A 13-shot game is noise. 5/13 carries a 95% Wilson interval of 18-64%; 2/5 from three is 12-77%; ten games (50/130) only narrow to 31-47%. Over half of the between-player variation in NBA 3P% within a season is chance, and 3P% needs roughly 750 attempts to stabilise (Daly-Grafstein & Bornn). Per-game reports should show intervals and grade decisions, never lead with FG%.
  2. Grade shots by expected points, not make/miss. Synergy Shot Quality is a per-league, per-shot-type logistic model (distance, guarded/unguarded, catch-and-shoot vs off the dribble, play type, sub-5 s clock) that deliberately ignores who is shooting; Shot Making = actual PPS minus expected PPS (Sportradar). The same split tells a 5/13 night apart as bad selection or bad making.
  3. The shot he takes most is the worst shot in the reference tables. NBA 2024-25: catch-and-shoot threes 37.4% (1.12 PPS), pull-up threes 32.9% (0.99), pull-up twos 42.5% (0.85), paint 57.1% (NBA.com). Touch time under 2 s ran .54 eFG% vs about .44 for 2-6 s and 6+ s in a Dec 2014 snapshot that includes transition, so it is descriptive, not causal (Shea via Wayback). The lever is his share of long-hold, multi-dribble attempts, trended across games, not a ban on pull-ups.
  4. Process metrics move in 3-4 games; percentages take months. A share-type metric at 13 attempts has the same per-game noise as FG%, but moving the pull-up share from ~60% to ~30% is a 30-point shift that clears a Wilson band in about 40-50 attempts, while a 5-point FG% gain needs 300+ attempts (binomial reasoning; no source gives stabilisation points for process metrics, Basketball Index).
  5. Fewer, cued clips beat the whole reel. Video feedback appears more effective than verbal alone only when it carries short verbal cues; video-only produced no improvement in one adult study (Mödinger et al.). The 2026 Frontiers framework prescribes 10-20 s clips, one attentional cue per viewing, at most two replays, and a "Strength: X; Improvement: Y" prompt (Tannoubi et al.). The current 27-clip reel with "+2 PTS" pop-ups is the format the review flags as possibly ineffective.
  6. Three teaching points, 10-15 minutes, corrections followed by the same action done right. Each number is single-sourced (PGC, Better Basketball, Hudl's one-on-one piece) and all from youth coaching (PGC, Hudl). No sport trial supports a fixed 3:1 positive ratio; Wooden's coded acts were 6.9% praise and ~75% information, but feedback after good trials does improve retention, so valence matters alongside content (Marshall Memo on Gallimore & Tharp).
  7. Keep the best-plays reel separate from the corrective edit. Raw self-footage produced more negative than positive self-evaluation (15.6% vs 6%); a best-clips edit reversed it and raised positive-feeling statements from 2.5% to 15.9% (n=10, exploratory) (Middlemas 2017). Self-modeling effects are dose-dependent and fade within a week, so the reel must be re-cut every game (Wang et al. 2026).
  8. Make him guess before the reveal. Learners required to estimate their own error before seeing the result retained best; those given the result without estimating retained worst (Guadagnoli & Kohl 2001). Coaches overestimate their own good feedback by 5-40%, so the absence of a coach costs less than it looks, provided the system shows clip plus number (Mason, Farrow & Hattie 2020).
  9. Cue the ball, rim and floor, not the elbow. External-focus cues beat body-part cues across ~80 experiments (performance g about 0.26, learning g about 0.58); in basketball the evidence is thin (one 10-shot free-throw study, 25.6% vs 20.9%), and "more distal is better" is a separate small effect (Wulf 2013 review; Chua 2021 meta-analysis). Modest effect, zero cost: reword every caption.
  10. Keep the fun outputs number-free. Measuring an enjoyable activity raised output but lowered enjoyment and continued engagement in six experiments, and the penalty vanished only when the activity was framed as work (Etkin 2016). The Top 10 and shared recap stay stat-free; the quantified layer is a separate "training review" he opens by choice.
  11. The wide camera measures time, position and ball flight; it does not measure form. Pose from a sideline phone gives usable shoulder angles but coarse elbow (RMSE 16-21 deg) and useless wrist angles (RMSE 63-85 deg) even with the subject filling the frame (Waterloo thesis); at 100-160 px, skeletons are visual aids only. Hold time (+/-33 ms), dribble count, take-off position and short/long miss class are the trustworthy outputs.
  12. Fix form in practice, with feedback inside a second; use the game report for decisions. Noah's founder: fine-motor feedback later than ~0.5 s is "essentially worthless" (Noah/Sheppard). A second phone at 240 fps, side-on, 2-4 m away, is the only way to see the July findings.
  13. Rebuilding an automated shot is a staged, up-to-12-month project in which performance drops first and the old pattern returns under pressure unless practice is representative (Carson & Collins, Five-A). One change at a time; in-season tweaks only.
  14. Spaced beats massed (d = 0.46 overall, 0.97 for simple psychomotor tasks, shrinking with complexity), so 5-minute form blocks on 3-4 days beat one 25-minute session (Donovan & Radosevich 1999).
  15. Legs are a shooting input and a depreciating one. Release height is the kinematic variable tied to makes (Amaro 2025); lower-limb power falls about 1%/year after 30 (Alvero-Cruz 2021); strength training cuts sports injuries to under a third (RR 0.315) while stretching does nothing (RR 0.963) (Lauersen 2014).

Per-game roll-ups

Tendencies: shot mix by shot_class and zone; rim+three share ("Moreyball rate", pro teams run 75-86%, Hudl EuroLeague); share of attempts with 3+ dribbles or 6+ s hold; catch-and-shoot share; paint-touch share of possessions (D1 teams ~40%, 1.06 PPP with a paint touch vs 0.71 without); drive direction split; decision latency histogram. Shot quality: expected points created, actual points, Shot Making delta, mean shot grade of attempts and of makes separately. Decision quality: count of possessions ending in a grade <=4 shot, pass-outs of good-not-great shots (credited as positives), turnovers per possession used. Rim outcomes: short/long/left/right mix across the last 3-4 games (direction of misses is informative at small samples; percentages are not). Fatigue split: game 1 vs game 3 for hold time, arc, short-miss share; a 12-minute simulated-game protocol flattened guards' entry angle 45.2 to 43.4 deg and slowed release 0.6 to 0.7 s (Bourdas 2024). A tendency is promoted to a "weakness" only with 3+ supporting clips across games (the NBA trainer's volume rule, Lowman).

Order of the review

  1. Before opening anything, he types three strengths and three fixes from memory (self-SWOT first; reduces anxiety and gives a self-awareness score over time, Lowman).
  2. Two process goals chosen before the game are scored first, with their clips.
  3. Three cards, most important first: each one clip pair (the possession as played, then the same action done right, by him if available), one external cue of <=20 words, one drill with a make target. Corrections grouped and immediately followed by the correct version (Better Basketball).
  4. Strengths appear as "keep doing" instructions with their own clip, not as praise.
  5. Everything else collapsed. Total viewing 10-15 minutes. "Film to floor": review in the slot before the next practice or game.
  6. The best-plays reel is a separate artefact, watched before the next game, not after this one.

3. Feedback that changes behaviour

Nearly all of this evidence comes from children, students or novices on closed skills; no study covers an adult coaching himself from his own game film (Mödinger 2021). The directions agree. Read the effect sizes as ceilings.

3.1 Cue design

Cues aimed at the ball, rim or floor beat cues aimed at the wrist, elbow or hand: across about 80 experiments the external-focus advantage is g ≈ 0.26 for performance and 0.58 for learning, unmoderated by skill level (Chua et al. 2021). Basketball evidence is thin: one within-subject study got 25.6% vs 20.9% free-throw accuracy focusing on the hoop rather than the wrist (Zachry 2005 in Wulf 2013). Wording alone moves form: identical feedback phrased about "the sneakers" instead of "feet, hips, knees and shoulders" scored 5.95 vs 5.05 on transfer form, accuracy unchanged, 12 children per group (Wulf 2010).

Caption rules, relayed from Blischke 1999 as background rather than as a review finding: about 20 words, picture above text, one parameter per correction (Mödinger 2021), shaped verb + preposition + landmark (Winkelman summary). A clip without a cue may do nothing: video-only feedback produced no gain in an adult strength study where verbal and video-plus-cues did (Rucci & Tomporowski 2010, in Mödinger). One analogy beats a rule list: analogy learners of a basketball shot matched explicit learners on retention and held up under pressure while the explicit group deteriorated (Lam, Maxwell & Masters 2009); the Five-A model uses holistic rhythm words because part-skill cues "fragment the movement under stress" (Carson & Collins 2016). Rehearsed instructional cue words help fine skills (self-talk ES .48) (Hatzigeorgiadis 2011, APS summary). So "elbow in" and "silent guide hand" stay in the practice notes; the game-day cue is one external phrase or one analogy.

3.3 Immediate or delayed

Mechanics and decisions need different loops. Noah's founder calls fine-motor feedback later than about half a second "essentially worthless" (Noah, Sheppard profile; vendor claim), and the relayed modelling parameter is feedback within 45 s of the rep (Mödinger 2021). A next-day report cannot retrain a release; it sets the practice agenda and works on shot selection and hold time. For decisions, delay plus questions is the better-evidenced route: swimmers whose coach withheld feedback and asked questions improved technique and race times more than controls (Chambers & Vickers 2006, summary; abstract-level), and 11 U13 players who reviewed their clips with questioning made more successful decisions (p = .006) and executions (p = .011) at post-test, gone by retention; the controls also watched video, so questioning was the active ingredient (Gil-Arias 2019). Praise is not the lever: 6.9% of Wooden's 2,326 coded acts were praise and about 75% carried information (Tharp & Gallimore summary). Write strengths as "keep doing this", with a clip.

4. Drill library mapped to weakness types

Each weakness gets two or three drills with a dose, one cue and a test the tracker can score. Cues point at the ball, rim or floor where the source gives one: across about 80 experiments, cues aimed at the movement effect beat body-part cues for novices and experienced players alike, with a modest effect (learning g about 0.58) (Chua et al. 2021). Mechanics are coached in practice; in games the attention goes to the target (Nordland). Rebuilding an automated shot is a staged project of up to 12 months: performance drops first, and the old pattern returns under pressure unless the new one is practised in game-like conditions (Carson & Collins 2016). No adult-recreational norms exist for any test below; the high-school numbers are ceilings and the first score is the baseline (Decathlon, Teach Hoops). Rows 7-9 target the July 2026 findings (two-hand push, elbow flare, centred ball, dip-pause-push).

Practice design. Blocked form shooting from one spot is fine for acquisition. 6. What a single wide camera can and cannot measure

One ground-level phone at mid-court, 1080p at 30 fps, players 100-160 px tall. The honest split is three buckets: what the pipeline can measure with a known error, what Claude should judge by eye from cropped frames, and the July form findings, which this footage will never recover.

6.1 Subject size sets the ceiling

Pose accuracy degrades steadily as the person crop shrinks and collapses near 64x48 px: top-down HRNet-W32 scores 75.8 AP at 384x288, 61.5 at 128x96 and 29.7 at 64x48 on COCO (Wang et al.). Barak's players at native resolution (~128x96 crops) sit in the moderate zone. The damage is self-inflicted: YOLO's default imgsz=640 scales the 1920-wide frame to one third, so a 130 px player becomes ~43 px, below the collapse point; YOLO-Pose itself falls from 64.9 AP at 1280 input to 57.5 at 640 and 44.9 at 384 (YOLO-Pose). Fixes: infer at imgsz 1280-1920, tile with SAHI, or crop the tracked shooter from the original frame and run a top-down model on the upscaled crop, the reason top-down beat bottom-up on far players in the padel study (Javadiha et al.).

Even then, keypoints are not measurements. With a close camera and the subject filling the frame, upper-arm angles reach ~7° RMSE against Vicon, elbow 16-21°, wrist 63-85°, the wrist errors largely from motion blur (Waterloo thesis). Camera position alone moved pose F1 from 0.96 to 0.59, and the side of the body away from the camera fails first: left elbow F1 0.69, left hip 0.66, index fingers 0.47-0.63 (Thai camera-position study). On game footage, draw hips, knees, ankles, shoulders and elbows as a visual aid and print no joint angles.

  9. Keep the dip; target the pause. In 36 elite shooters, dipped catch-and-shoot reps scored higher at all four distances (high-school group at 6.75 m: 4.29 vs 3.47 on a 1-6 scale), including university habitual non-dippers. The study did not test pauses, so "delete the stop at the set point" is a coaching inference, and the dip costs some shot speed. Penner 2021

  10. Split every stat by game 1 vs game 3. After 12 minutes of simulated play, top-league guards' entry angle fell 45.2° to 43.4°, release time rose 0.6 to 0.7 s and makes dropped 51.8% to 44.1% (accuracy CIs include zero). If the late numbers sag, prescribe legs and shot selection, not form. Bourdas 2024

  11. On a teammate's shot, go to the nearest elbow. Coaching rule of thumb: about 70% of wing and corner misses go long to the opposite side and 3-point misses fly over the bigs; crash only a true 50/50 ball, otherwise sprint back. No data behind the 70%, but it turns his loose-ball instinct into a position the tracker can grade every game. Coach's Clipboard

  12. Score arc as a 40-47° band, and check the rim first. Noah says 45°, Spalding 44-50°, elite youth made threes entered at 44.0° vs 42.4° missed, and independent physics gives a 52° free-throw launch, about a 40° entry by the gap-fill's own calculation; what every source agrees on is depth consistency. Outdoor double rims have less give (no measured comparison), so calibrate make/miss on 50 known shots at his rim before crediting back-rim misses as near-makes (GF). Tran & Silverberg 2008, Spalding arc data, Croatian entry-angle study, double rims
