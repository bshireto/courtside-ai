Where to put the camera

Same spot every game, so the recap tools can reuse the court map. Phone horizontal, 1080p (60 fps if the phone offers it), no zoom, exposure and focus locked.

Half court

One basket. Camera beside the court, level with the free-throw line, looking at the rim from about 30 degrees.

  1. Side of the court, level with the free-throw line.
  2. About 3 m back from the sideline.
  3. Lens 2.5–3 m high: tripod on a bench, fence or pole. Height is what lets me see the ball drop through the net.
  4. Aim at the rim, turned about 30 degrees toward the basket. The rim sits in the upper third of the frame, about a third in from the side.
  5. Whole three-point arc in frame, both corners included. That's how 2s and 3s get told apart.
  6. Plain background behind the rim: a dark wall or tent, not the floodlights.

Full court

Both baskets. Camera at the centre line, far back and high, seeing both baselines.

  1. Centre line, on the side of the court.
  2. 4–5 m back from the sideline, far enough that both baselines fit in frame.
  3. As high as possible (3 m+). Height keeps the far rim readable.
  4. Level horizon, both rims at similar height in the frame.
  5. If you can't get far enough back, use a second phone at the other end rather than zooming out.

Avoid

What made the first game hard to analyse.

Don'tUnder a basket, shooting lengthwiseThe far rim shrinks to about 15 pixels, so makes and misses can't be read. This happened in the second half of 23.09.
Don'tMoving the camera mid-sessionEach new position needs a new court map. If you must, stop recording, move, start a new clip.
Don'tPeople standing in frontKeep the sideline in front of the camera clear. Bags, benches and spectators block the rim.
Don'tZoom, vertical, auto-exposureDigital zoom loses detail, and exposure pumps when the floodlights enter the frame.

Before you press record

CheckBoth rims fully visibleIncluding the whole net below them.
CheckWhole three-point arc visibleFor every basket you're recording.
CheckPhone plugged in, storage freeA 30-minute 1080p game is about 4 GB.
